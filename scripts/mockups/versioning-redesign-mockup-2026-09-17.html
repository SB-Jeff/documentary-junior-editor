3 · Viewing a past step: read-only, with a clear way back

Clicking View shows that step in the Timeline without touching your working state. The banner says what you are looking at. Restore copies it forward as a new step on the edit; Back returns to now. Autosave pauses while viewing, so a glance at history can never overwrite your work.

4 · Versions and Save as: alternatives you iterate on separately

5 · What changes underneath

The user-facing simplification is possible because one file per edit becomes the single truth for both you and the agent.

On disk, per project

handoffs/keystone-2026/
  edits/
    main/
      current.json      ← always-saved working state (you + agent read this)
      edit.json         ← name, created-from, timestamps
      steps/
        001-claude-first-assembly.json
        002-claude-act1-initial-suggestion.json
        003-jeff-act1-assembly-pass.json
        004-claude-act1-cut-proposal.json
        005-jeff-act1-revision.json
        006-claude-act2-proposal.json
    tighter-cut/
      current.json
      edit.json         ← forked_from: main @ step 006
      steps/ ...
  editing-versions/v1.json v2.json …   ← migrated in as steps of main
  editing-versions/checkpoints/       ← migrated in as steps
  editing-versions/<named>.json       ← migrated in as edits
  viewer-state.json                   ← replaced by edits/<edit>/current.json

The agent's contract (SKILL-edit)

  • Read the open edit's current.json at the top of every turn, exactly as it reads viewer-state.json today.
  • When you say "move to Act N" (or otherwise hand off), write your state as the next step, labelled "Jeff: …" from the conversation.
  • When it seeds a proposal, write current.json and add a step "Claude: …". No separate bake, no separate checkpoint.
  • Never rebuild the page to deliver a proposal. The viewer re-reads current.json when the agent writes it, so the baked page can no longer be older than your work.
  • Follows the open edit. If Tighter cut is open, its steps go there.

Goes away

  • Numbered rounds, named cuts, and checkpoints as three separate kinds of thing.
  • The dirty dot, the "Save changes" overwrite, the restore-on-load banner.
  • The build-time list of versions. The viewer reads edits live from the app server.

Stays

  • The app server, the 3-tier writer, the "offline" self-heal, Export, project chat.
  • Old projects still open: their rounds and checkpoints become steps; their named cuts become edits.

One act, one round-trip
