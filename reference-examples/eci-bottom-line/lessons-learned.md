# Lessons Learned — ECI "The Recommendation" (eci-bottom-line)
## Completed: August 2026 · Project Type: B2B Testimonial (peer-recommendation capstone; video 4 of 4)
## Subjects: Jordan (lead, 5 of 9 quotes), Drew, Henry (selection story only), Vlad (cut entirely)

### Summary — the series' one big miss
1:25, 9 quotes, 40 clips. Jeff assembled this final DIRECTLY in FCP from the multicams; only 3 of
the Edit Agent's 13 Round-3 entries survived (#106, #121, #78 — 23%, vs 88%/100%/79% on the three
siblings). The concept had already pivoted twice (narrative 3-act → "greatest hits" endorsement
montage → four-beat spine), and the agent's four-beat build still missed what Jeff actually wanted.
His final: who Concept Labs is (#174) → operations are complex (#106) → at scale the old way stops
working (#63) → the professionalize mission (#102) → how we landed on Deacom (#121) → the system
lets us scale (#104) → PO-tracking demonstration (#37) → ERP as the amplifying foundation (#107) →
single button: "I would ABSOLUTELY recommend DEACOM" (#78).

### Where the agent went wrong (each logged for next time)
1. **Endorsement stacking.** Read "greatest hits of recommendations" literally and programmed ~8
   recommend-adjacent quotes from all four speakers. Jeff used exactly ONE explicit recommendation,
   as the closing button, after an earned setup. Repetition of the same claim dilutes it.
   (Promoted into SKILL-edit.md under assertion-vs-demonstration.)
2. **Keyword-matched the beats instead of reading for craft.** For "Deacom is the best ERP" the
   agent picked quotes that SAY best (#148/#154/#132); Jeff picked quotes that SHOW it — the
   selection story (#121), the daily PO walkthrough (#37), the foundation mechanism (#107). Fourth
   consecutive final proving assertion-vs-demonstration; now promoted to the skill.
3. **Over-obeyed its own ownership ledger.** #63/#102/#104/#107 material was stood down as
   growth-owned; Jeff freely reprised ~36 seconds of footage already shipped inside "Built to
   Grow" (plus ~2.5s of Henry from quality-trust). A capstone may recap — mid-series videos may
   not. Ledger's job on a finale: flag reprises for a knowing choice, not pre-exclude them.
4. **Un-learned the wrong lesson on Henry.** Original plan: Henry never near the recommendation
   (he hedges). Agent reversed that for the montage concept and featured the hedge (#133). Jeff's
   final vindicates the original instinct — Henry appears only in the how-we-chose story, and no
   hedged recommendation made the cut. Vlad's veteran's-verdict pair also went unused: zero Vlad.
5. **Micro-cutting is Jeff's finishing signature at its strongest here** — avg clip 1.9s, heavy
   intercutting within sentences (40 clips over 9 quotes). Paper cuts should hand him whole
   thoughts; he Frankensteins the rhythm himself.

### What worked
- The pool and per-speaker tagging held up — everything he used was catalogued, incl. orphan-region
  material; nothing had to be re-transcribed.
- The pipeline artifacts made the post-hoc reconstruction and sibling-overlap audit trivial.

### Rule that emerged
On a short capstone/recap video, expect Jeff to cherry-pick directly in FCP. The agent's highest-
value contribution there is a well-ordered MENU (per-beat candidates with segment addresses) plus
the sibling-overlap ledger — not a locked paper cut. Offer the menu before building a round.
